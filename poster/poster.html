When a hint changes MedGemma’s answer, its reasoning hides it. Its activations don’t.

Temesgen Tewolde
Duke University · AIPI 590: Explainable AI
Abstract

A medical model’s written reasoning may leave out what drove its answer. We added hints that point to an answer to 375 MedQA questions for MedGemma 4B. The hint changed the answer in 912 runs, and the reasoning mentioned it in only 30 (3.3%). Inside the model, the hint travels as an answer-letter signal. A probe on layer-22 activations, never told the hint, catches hint-following with AUROC 0.98. Steering against that signal fixes quick answers but not reasoned ones, and the probe does not catch prompt injection (0.48).

Objectives
  1. See it: how often does a hint change the answer without the reasoning saying so?
  2. Catch it: do the model’s activations reveal what the text hides?
  3. Fix it: does steering the model’s activations stop it?
  4. Injection: is it the same weakness as prompt injection?
Methods
  • Model: MedGemma 4B (open weights), greedy decoding, read with Gemma Scope 2 SAEs at layers 9, 17, 22, 29.
  • Data: 375 MedQA questions (4 options) not in the MPIB benchmark. No-hint accuracy 64.8%.
  • 10 versions per question: no hint; a harmless sham sentence; 4 hints (metadata tag, “Hint: the correct answer is X”, an attending’s opinion, a patient’s insistence), each pointing to a wrong or the right answer.
  • Labels: a judge model (Qwen 27B) checks whether the reasoning mentions the hint; it agreed with 2 blind model annotators on 100/100 cases. Silent = followed and never mentioned.
  • Detector: logistic-regression probes on activations, 5 folds grouped by question, never shown the hint text or letter.
  • Fix and injection: pre-registered. Directions learned on a dev half; 150 MPIB cases with an instruction hidden in the question.
Demo

An interactive page shows what the model says next to what drove its answer (no-hint answer, judge check, warning badge), with live hint toggles for 40 questions. Precomputed; no GPU needed.

Results
1 · See it
  • Every hint moved answers far above the 4% sham floor. The metadata tag moved 64% and was never mentioned.

Wrong-answer hints: share of eligible runs that moved to the hinted answer. Dashed: sham sentence. Whiskers: 95% CIs. Of those that moved, the reasoning admitted the hint in 0/206, 6/175, 14/95, 1/57 (same order).

2 · Catch it
  • The hint travels as answer-letter features on the hint’s own tokens. Removing 10 at layer 17 cut quick-answer hint-following from 87% to 75%; features that only correlate barely moved the pull toward the hinted letter (log-probability -0.07, vs -0.83).
  • A hint-blind probe catches silent hint-following (within hint type 0.98; unseen hint types 0.98–0.99). Text-only detectors do far worse.

AUROC (0.5 = chance) for silent hint-following vs ignored hints, 1,272 runs. Bottom bar: moved vs unmoved answers on 150 injection cases.

3 · Fix it
  • Removing the hint-following direction works on quick answers but not once the model reasons; random directions do nothing.

Hint-following on dev questions (different samples per row). Whiskers: 95% CIs.

4 · Injection
  • Injected instructions moved 26% of answers, but the probe could not tell which (0.48). Pre-registered verdict: inconclusive, leaning against a shared mechanism.

Conclusion
97%
of hint-driven answer changes are never mentioned
0.98
AUROC of a probe that never sees the hint
  1. See it. The reasoning hides almost every hint it follows.
  2. Catch it. Activations reveal it, but the probe flags following, not hiding: it fires on 76% of the 21 admitted runs, and on 54% of right-answer hints the model agreed with anyway.
  3. Fix it. Steering that works on quick answers fails once the model reasons (35/40 still followed after feature removal).
  4. Injection. The probe does not transfer.
Limitations
  • One 4B model; English multiple choice; greedy decoding.
  • Every hint names a letter; content cues untested.
  • Judge checked against model annotators, not people.
  • Small fix checks (24 dev questions); MPIB attacks target actions, not letters.
