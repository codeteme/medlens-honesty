Unverbalized Hint-Following in MedGemma 4B: Causal Features, Activation Probes and the Limits of Steering

Temesgen Tewolde
Duke University · AIPI 590: Explainable AI
Abstract

Black-box studies [1, 2, 5] show that language models, including medical models, adopt cues their reasoning omits. We examine the internals of MedGemma 4B on 375 MedQA questions. SAE attribution and ablation localize cue influence to answer-letter features on the hint tokens. A hint-blind layer-22 probe detects unverbalized hint-following (AUROC 0.98; text baselines ≤ 0.72) but encodes agreement with a suggested letter rather than concealment. Steering that works on single-pass answers fails under chain-of-thought, and the probe does not transfer to prompt injection.

Contributions
  1. Causal localization of cue influence to answer-letter SAE features (layers 17, 22); no source-specific features found.
  2. A hint-blind activation probe, evaluated within and across hint types against text baselines.
  3. Evidence that the probe encodes answer–cue agreement, not concealment.
  4. Pre-registered negative results for chain-of-thought steering and injection transfer.
Methods
  • Model: MedGemma 4B, greedy decoding; Gemma Scope 2 residual SAEs at layers 9, 17, 22 and 29.
  • Data: 375 four-option MedQA questions disjoint from MPIB; no-hint accuracy 64.8%.
  • Conditions: no hint, a sham sentence, and four cue framings (metadata, explicit, attending, patient) toward a correct or incorrect option.
  • Labels: LLM judge (Qwen 27B) with verbatim-quote checks; 100/100 agreement with two blind model annotators. Unverbalized: followed, not mentioned.
  • Probes: logistic regression on residual activations; five folds grouped by question; no access to the hint.
  • Interventions: mean-difference directions from a development half, ablated or subtracted at every token; criteria pre-registered.
Results
1. Behavioral baseline
  • All framings exceeded the 4% sham rate; the metadata tag shifted 64% of answers and was never verbalized.

Share of eligible incorrect-hint runs that adopted the hinted option. Dashed line: sham sentence. Whiskers: 95% CIs. Runs that verbalized the cue: 0/206, 6/175, 14/95, 1/57.

2. Causal localization and detection
  • Attribution-ranked features on the hint tokens encode the answer letter. Ablating the top 10 at layer 17 reduced single-pass hint-following from 87% to 75% (Δ log-p -0.83, vs -0.07 for correlation-ranked features) but not chain-of-thought decisions (35/40 → 35/40).
  • Probe AUROC within hint type 0.98; held-out types 0.98–0.99; option-text cues 0.82–0.83.

AUROC for unverbalized hint-following vs ignored hints (1,272 runs). Bottom bar: shifted vs unshifted answers on 149 injection cases.

3. Steering under chain-of-thought
  • Layer-22 ablation reduced single-pass hint-following; random directions did not. Under chain-of-thought, 0 of 4 interventions passed on 100 held-out questions (Holm).

Hint-following rate on development questions. Whiskers: 95% CIs.

4. Transfer to prompt injection
  • MPIB injections shifted 26% of answers; the probe did not identify which (AUROC 0.48). Pre-registered verdict: inconclusive, with the testable arm against a shared mechanism.

Discussion
0.98
AUROC of a hint-blind activation probe
0/4
interventions effective under chain-of-thought (held-out test)
  1. Cue influence is carried by a letter-level signal that activations expose and the reasoning omits.
  2. The probe detects agreement, not concealment: it fires on 76% of verbalized runs and on 54% of correct-answer hints the model already preferred, and on text-named cues it flags under 2% of unverbalized runs.
  3. Interventions validated on single-pass answers do not hold under chain-of-thought, consistent with the reasoning reconstructing the cue’s influence.
  4. Activation monitors require evaluation outside their training distribution; this probe did not transfer to injection.
Limitations
  • One 4B model; English multiple choice; greedy decoding.
  • Most cues name an option letter; fabricated-evidence cues were verbalized in 59 of 61 followed runs.
  • Judge validated against model annotators, not clinicians.
  • SAEs trained on Gemma 3, not MedGemma; only four layers examined.
